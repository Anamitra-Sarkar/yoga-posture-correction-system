\begin{abstract}
Self-guided yoga can cause injury, and the camera-based coaches published in 2025--2026 share open problems: accuracy is reported on random splits of small image sets, joints the camera cannot see are still judged, one body model serves everyone, generated advice is not screened, and systems either upload video or need extra hardware. We present AsanaAI, a yoga posture coach that runs in a web browser on commodity phones and laptops and sends only 33 body-landmark coordinates, never images, to a small CPU server. It combines supervision from instructor videos verified by three independent signals (speech, biomechanical rules, steadiness), a name-and-veto cascade of two 0.36\,M-parameter residual MLPs that can abstain, a 0.89\,M-parameter skeleton-graph network that separates held poses from transitions, visibility-aware scoring that declines to judge hidden joints, a personal range-of-motion profile, and a safety-screened English/Hindi/Bengali guidance pipeline. We evaluate only on data the models never trained on. The cascade names the pose on 78.9\% of 1{,}685 held-out photos with a 20.8\% false-alarm rate on other poses and seven poses at recall and precision $\ge0.70$, against 36.9\%, 78.3\% and none for the rule engine alone; a random forest on the same data is stronger on photos from the training sources (85.3\%) but weakest on wild photos from another source (35.0\% against 55.3\%), so we report both. On held-out videos the skeleton-graph network reaches 78.0\% macro recall, but under one common recipe simpler sequence models, including an 86\,k-parameter MLP on window statistics, match or exceed it, so we do not claim that the graph is necessary. Server compute is 7--9\,ms per frame within 324\,MB, a mid-range phone extracts landmarks at 19--24 results/s, and eight of eleven poses pass an end-to-end photo test on the phone. We state the limitations: no live-person study, weak mountain, cobra and child's pose, and a form score that is blind to arm errors.
\end{abstract}
